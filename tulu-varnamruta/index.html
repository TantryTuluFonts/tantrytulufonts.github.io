Tuḷu Varṇāmr̥ta

A Prescriptive Primer to the Tuḷu Script, by Prahlad Prasad Tantry

Everything you need to read and write the traditional Tuḷu script, in one book.

Tuḷu Varṇāmr̥ta teaches the whole script, from vowels and consonants to conjuncts and Vedic symbols, with romanization beside every example. You do not need to piece it together from multiple sources, and you do not need to know any other Indic script. By the final chapter, you are reading a complete story in Tuḷu.

It also introduces a standard for writing Tuḷu, something the language has long lacked. The standard keeps the traditional script exactly as it is and still accommodates what modern Tuḷu needs.

A standard Tuḷu has long lacked

Despite its rich literary tradition and its large number of speakers, Tuḷu has never had a standardized orthography for its traditional script. Manuscripts, inscriptions and printed texts each follow their own conventions, shaped by region, by individual scribal habit, or by whatever source a writer happened to learn from. No two sources could be trusted to agree with each other.

Tuḷu Varṇāmr̥ta is offered as both a teaching tool and a proposed standard: one coherent, rule-governed system for writing Tuḷu, argued for on principled grounds and applied without exception. Read why it should be maintained.

What you will learn

  • The whole script. Vowels and vowel signs, consonant letters, and the complete varṇamālā in śikṣā order.
  • The logic of the letters. A classification of letters by the geometry of their terminal stroke (śirovarṇa and pucchavarṇa) that explains how signs, conjuncts and virāmas behave, so you learn rules instead of memorizing exceptions.
  • Conjuncts. A catalogue of saṃyuktākṣaras, built on that same logic.
  • Orthographic rules. The virāma, the repha, gemination, numerals and punctuation.
  • Modern Tuḷu. A chapter on loanword orthography, from Sanskrit through to English and other modern European languages.
  • Vedic marks. How the traditional script represents recited Sanskrit with its pitch accents.
  • Reading. Kaḍēto Pāṭha, a complete Tuḷu story, to test everything you have learned.

A blessing for the traditional script

What I especially admire about this work is its commitment to preserving the original form of the traditional Tuḷu script without any compromise. Our Śrī Maṭha does not support any simplification, change or unnecessary revision of the traditional Tuḷu script. It is our firm position that the script that has come to us through tradition should be preserved in its original form, taught to coming generations, and promoted. Following this very principle, the book's effort to standardize the use of the traditional Tuḷu script while retaining its original form is commendable. I wholeheartedly welcome this good work.

Śrī Śrī Vidyēśatīrtha Svāmiḷ, Pīṭhādhipati, Śrī Bhaṇḍārakēri Maṭha, Bārkūru. From the Foreword to Tuḷu Varṇāmr̥ta, translated from the Kannada.

Who it is for

Heritage learners

Many people grew up speaking Tuḷu but never learned to read it. The book assumes nothing and builds the script from first principles.

Linguists and researchers

Reliable script literacy is the first step to reading primary Tuḷu sources, and the book gives it.

Indologists

Get to script literacy for historical documents and regional records without learning the full language.

Typographers and font makers

Detail on letter structure, conjunct formation and orthographic reasoning for anyone building digital fonts for the script.

From the author

Today, I'm a software engineer who has been learning the Tuḷu script since the eighth grade. Growing up outside Tuḷunāḍụ kindled my curiosity about the script and its history, and I began piecing together what I could from documents, books, manuscripts, fonts, and whatever other sources I could find. Along the way, I began making fonts and other tools to make the script easier to use. This book is my attempt to put together the kind of book I wished I had when I first began learning the script.

Questions

Do I need to know another Indic script?

No. The book assumes only literacy in English and basic linguistic terms, and builds the Tuḷu script from the beginning. Knowing Kannada, Malayalam or Devanagari may speed things up, but it is never required.

Is it a Tuḷu language course?

No. It is a script primer. It does not teach spoken Tuḷu or its grammar. It teaches you to read any Tuḷu word written in the traditional script and pronounce it correctly.

Does it cover modern words?

Yes. The loanword chapter sets out how each layer of borrowing is written, from the oldest Sanskrit words to English and other modern European languages.
